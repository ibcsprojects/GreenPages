How to shorten wordy writing without losing the point

Most wordy writing is padding: long phrases that have a short equivalent, filler words, and sentences that say what the last one said. Cutting them saves paper and usually makes the writing clearer.

Six edits that work

  1. Swap padded phrases for short ones. “In order to” becomes “to”. “Due to the fact that” becomes “because”. “At this point in time” becomes “now”.
  2. Cut filler words. Words like really, actually, basically and extremely rarely add meaning. “This is really important” is just “This is important”.
  3. Drop throat-clearing openers. “It is important to note that the test is on Friday” is “The test is on Friday”.
  4. Remove doubled pairs. “Each and every” is “each”. “Past history” is “history”.
  5. Say it once. If two sentences make the same point, keep the better one.
  6. Lead with the point. Put the main idea first, and cut the sentences that only add detail the reader does not need.

Do it in seconds

The text checker on the SparePage home page does the first five for you. Paste your text, see every change it would make, and copy the shorter version. It works in your browser and nothing you paste is sent anywhere. It only handles English.

A word of caution

Shorter is not always better. Check that a cut did not remove a fact, a date or a name, especially when a tool suggests leaving out whole sentences.
