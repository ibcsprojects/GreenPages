How to reduce the page count of a Google Doc

You can usually take a page or more off a Google Doc without cutting anything that matters. Start by checking the count, then work through the changes below, biggest first.

Check where you are

Open Tools, then Word count (Ctrl+Shift+C, or Cmd+Shift+C on a Mac). It shows the number of pages. Check it again after each change.

What to change

  1. Margins. File, then Page setup. The default is 1 inch. 0.75 inch is still comfortable to read.
  2. Font size. Body text at 11 or 12 points is plenty. 14 points and up spills onto extra pages.
  3. Line and paragraph spacing. Format, then Line & paragraph spacing. Single or 1.15 is enough, and removing the space after paragraphs helps.
  4. Blank lines and page breaks. Turn on View, Show non-printing characters, and delete rows of empty paragraphs and page breaks that leave half-empty pages.
  5. Pictures. Click an image and drag a corner to make it smaller. A picture that fills most of a page also uses a lot of ink.
  6. Long lists. A list of short items can become a two-column table that takes a third of the space.
  7. Wordy text. Cut padded phrases and repeated sentences. This guide shows how.
  8. Print on both sides. It halves the sheets whatever the page count. Here is how.
Or let SparePage do it. It looks for steps 1 to 6 and the wordy text in step 7, lists each issue with what it saves, and applies the fix when you click. See how to install it.
